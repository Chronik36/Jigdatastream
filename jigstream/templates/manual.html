BotTrack manual

BotTrack reads your Gmail inboxes with an AI model that runs on your own Mac, and turns the emails into one list of orders (tracking, address, dates, shipping status) and one list of botting expenses (proxies, bots, cook groups, whop.com and so on). It also keeps your bot profiles, cards and email lists in one place, and reads your bots' checkout webhooks from Discord.

Use the search box on the left to jump to anything, for example tracking, app password or mass distribute. Every picture has orange numbers on it; the list under the picture says what each number is. Click a picture to see it bigger.

Everything stays on this Mac. Your mail is read here, the AI runs here, and nothing in BotTrack costs money.

Getting started

Installing BotTrack

  1. Download BotTrack.dmg from the BotTrack releases page.
  2. Open BotTrack.dmg and drag BotTrack into your Applications folder.
  3. The first time, right-click (or Control-click) BotTrack in Applications and choose Open, then Open again. macOS asks this once because BotTrack doesn't come from the App Store. After that it opens normally.
  4. BotTrack opens in its own window. Everything it needs (Python, the AI engine) is inside the app, so there is nothing else to install.

The first launch downloads the AI

On first launch BotTrack downloads its AI model, about 9 GB. A bar at the top of every page shows how far along it is, and the Local AI card at the bottom of the sidebar says Getting ready until it's done. You can look around and set things up while it downloads; syncing starts by itself as soon as the AI is ready.

{{ fig("ai-banner", "The AI download bar at the top of the page.", ["How much of the AI model has downloaded. When it reaches 100% the bar disappears."]) }}

Your first five minutes

  1. Go to Setup → Gmail inboxes and add each Gmail inbox you use for orders.
  2. If your bots post checkouts in Discord, connect them on Setup → Discord.
  3. Import your bots' profile exports on the Dashboard, or create profiles on the Profiles page.
  4. Open Orders and press Sync now. The first sync reads the last year of mail and can take a while.
  5. When something looks wrong, fix it. Correcting a status on Orders or labelling an email on Inbox teaches the AI.

What each status means

StatusMeaning
OrderedThe retailer confirmed the order but hasn't shipped it.
In transitIt shipped and is on the way.
Out for deliveryIt's on the truck today.
DeliveredThe retailer or carrier says it arrived.
Cancelled / ReturnedThe order was cancelled, returned or refunded.

Fixing a wrong status

Pick the right status in the Change status… dropdown under the status. BotTrack then searches your inboxes for that order's emails, re-reads them, and saves them as examples so the AI gets it right next time (see Training). A ✎ next to the status means you set it by hand; it stays that way until you change it again.

The dropdowns only show while Training mode is on (it is unless you switch it off).

Tracking links

Status comes from your emails, and nothing is paid for. Every 3 hours BotTrack also opens the USPS, UPS or FedEx tracking page for each open package in a hidden window on your Mac, reads the status, and shows the carrier's latest scan under the tracking number. No keys are needed; you can turn this off, or add optional free carrier keys, on Setup → Live package tracking. The tracking number links to the carrier's own public page (UPS, USPS, FedEx, DHL, OnTrac), using the link from the email when there is one. If the carrier isn't known, it opens a free page that works out the carrier from the number.

The Ship to column is the address from the email, and Inbox shows which of your inboxes the order came from.

The labels

Order placed, Shipped / In transit, Out for delivery, Delivered, Cancelled, Returned / refunded, Botting expense, Sale / payout and Not an order. When you label an email the AI re-reads it, compares its answer to yours, and saves the pair as a training example. The label is also applied: a Delivered label updates that order, a Botting expense label adds the receipt to Expenses, and a Sale / payout label adds the sale to the Profit goal tab. Use the Sales filter to find sale emails. Give it a minute, then refresh.

Label a handful of emails from your usual retailers and vendors early on. Five or more labelled emails also unlock Compare models.

Ledger

The Ledger puts every botting receipt next to the card charge that paid for it, using the statements you imported on Bank. Each row gets a status:

  • Matched: the receipt (or order) and the card charge agree.
  • Charge, no receipt: money left your card, but no email explains it. Use Match… to pick the receipt or order, add it as an expense, or Ignore it if it isn't botting.
  • Receipt, no charge: a receipt your imported cards never charged. If you paid with PayPal balance, crypto or a card you didn't import, choose it under Paid another way.
  • Close amount: a charge a few dollars off its receipt, from currency fees or tax added at checkout. Press Confirm if it's the same purchase, or Not this.
  • No statement yet: the receipt is from a date your statements don't cover, so there is nothing to check it against.

Profit goal

What you sold, what each flip made, and whether the month covers your botting expenses. Sales come from sale and payout emails (StockX, GOAT, eBay, Tradepost, Mercari, Vinted, Alias, and any marketplace you add), from payout deposits on your bank statements, or typed in with Add sale. A "you sold" email and its later "payout sent" email become one sale.

Each sale is tied to the order you bought the item with, so its profit is the payout minus what you paid. If there's no order (bought in store, or you'd rather use MSRP), type the cost. Break-even is when profit on flips covers the month's botting expenses and the renewals still due; your goal is the profit you want on top of that.

Importing a statement

  1. On your bank or card website, open the account's activity and choose Download or Export.
  2. Pick CSV, or Quicken / QFX or OFX if it's offered. Chase, Amex, Capital One, Citi, Discover and most other banks work.
  3. In BotTrack, choose the file (or several), type a name for the account, and press Import.

Importing the same file twice adds nothing, so it's safe to download overlapping months. If the amounts come out the wrong way round, import again with Charges show as negative or positive chosen instead of detect automatically.

How charges are matched

A charge is matched when it's the same amount, to the cent, as an order or receipt dated from a week before to two days after it, and the store's name is in the bank's description (or it's the only one that fits). When it isn't sure, it leaves the charge for you.

  • Match opens a search of your orders and receipts so you can pick the right one.
  • Ignore is for charges that have nothing to do with botting, like groceries or rent.
  • Or add it as a botting expense (in the Match window) is for a proxy, bot or server charge that never sent an email receipt.
  • Match again re-checks every charge you haven't decided yourself.
A choice you make by hand is never undone by a later import or sync.

Live bank sync with Plaid (optional)

Instead of downloading files, BotTrack can read charges from your bank by itself through Plaid, the service most finance apps use. You make your own free Plaid account, and you sign in to your bank in Plaid's window, so BotTrack never sees your bank password. Setting it up takes about 10 minutes.

  1. On the Bank page press Set up live sync →.
  2. Create a free account at dashboard.plaid.com, open Developers → Keys, and copy the client_id and the Sandbox secret.
  3. Paste them in, keep Sandbox chosen and press Save. Try it with Plaid's test bank: username user_good, password pass_good.
  4. For your real bank, ask for Production access in the Plaid dashboard. Once it's approved, paste the Production secret with Production chosen, then press Link a bank.
{{ fig("bank-plaid", "The Plaid setup page.", ["Step 1: the instructions for getting your free keys, and where to paste them.", "Sandbox for the test bank, Production for your real bank."]) }}

The first pull brings in the last two years of charges, then new ones every few hours. Pending charges wait until they post. Your Plaid keys and bank link are stored encrypted on this Mac.

Stuck

Sidebar → Bot tools → Stuck

A bot says "success" as soon as the site takes the order, but many orders get cancelled later. This page checks every successful checkout against your order emails and bank refunds, and lists the ones that stuck: no cancel email or refund came after them.

  • A checkout is matched to an order email by its order number. When the webhook has no order number, it's matched to an order from the same site within a few days of the checkout, sent to the account email the bot used (the email's To address). Failing that, an order matched to the same profile.
  • Order email, no cancel: the order was confirmed, shipped or delivered by email.
  • No email found: no order email matched yet. Sync your inboxes first; if it still shows here, the emails may have gone to an inbox you haven't added, or the webhook's site or profile doesn't match the email.
  • Cancelled: the ones a cancel or refund email caught, or a refund on your bank or card. Click the status to open the order.
  • Bank refund: when you import statements or link a bank on the Bank page, a refund from the same store for the order total (or the price the bot reported, plus up to 15% for tax and shipping), within 10 days of the checkout, marks it cancelled. If the profile's card and the bank account both show their last 4 digits, they have to match.

Products

Sidebar → Bot tools → Products

Helps you decide what to buy. Describe the kind of product you're after and BotTrack searches what Target and a list of Shopify stores (Kith, Undefeated, Bodega, Concepts and more) sell right now, showing each product's real SKU, price and stock at every store that lists it. The local AI only turns your description into search words and drops results that don't fit; it never supplies a price or SKU, and anything a store doesn't list shows as not listed.   3. Press Update on a product (or Update all prices) to look up resale prices.
  4. Open Details to add store prices from a product link, type in prices for sites that can't be read, or find similar products.

Gmail inboxes

Gmail needs an app password for each inbox: a 16-letter code that lets BotTrack read mail without your real password. It can only read, never send or delete.

  1. Turn on 2-Step Verification for the Google account, if it isn't on already (the link is on the Setup page).
  2. Open App passwords (also linked), type a name like "BotTrack", and press Create.

Using BotTrack on your phone iPhone & Android

Your phone reaches BotTrack on your Mac from anywhere, at home or on mobile data, with nothing to install on the Mac and no account. It's free, and it only works while BotTrack is open on the Mac.

  1. On the Mac, open Setup → Phone access, tick Let my phones connect and press Save. A QR code appears after a few seconds (the first time takes about a minute).

Phones stay connected when BotTrack updates, is closed and opened again, or either app is closed. The link only changes when the Mac restarts: the Android app finds the new one by itself, and on an iPhone you scan the code again.

Training

Sidebar → Tracking → Training

Teach the AI on this Mac any job, not just reading emails: sorting Discord checkout messages, pulling the size and price out of a pasted product page, tagging raffle results, whatever comes up. Each job is a task. The built-in Email reader is one too.

{{ fig("training", "The Training page.", [ "Email reader: the built-in task that reads your inbox. It learns from Inbox labels and order corrections.", "Your own tasks, with how many examples each has and whether it's trained.", "New task: a name, plain instructions, and whether answers are plain text or JSON fields."]) }}

Teaching a task

Examples are the teaching: an input and the answer you want. The newest ones (up to 6) are shown to the AI before each new input, so a new example helps on the very next run. The fastest way to collect them is to run the task, fix its answer, and press Save as example.

{{ fig("training-task", "A task's page.", [ "Try it: paste something and press Run to see the AI's answer. Fix it and save it as an example.", "Add an example by hand.", "Import many at once: a CSV with input and output columns, or a JSONL file.", "Run over a file: answer up to 1,000 inputs from a CSV or text file and download the results.", "Train: pick a 4B or 8B model and train it on your examples (needs 10 or more).", "Trained models: which one is in use. Use, switch back, or delete any of them."]) }}

Training a model (fine-tuning)

Examples guide the AI but don't change it. Train does: BotTrack fine-tunes a small abliterated Qwen3 model on your examples, right on this Mac with Apple's MLX engine, for free. It makes a small add-on file (a LoRA adapter, tens of MB) and never changes the original model. A trained task answers with its own model from then on.

50 or more examples train much better than 10. Delete examples with wrong answers first: the model learns exactly what you give it.
On a 16 GB Mac this works for 4B models and usually 8B. The 14B model is too big to train, so a trained task runs on a smaller model that knows your task well. While training runs, syncing pauses and the main AI is unloaded to make room. The first run downloads the model (2 to 5 GB).

Questions and fixes

No orders show up

  • Check that an inbox is listed on Setup → Gmail inboxes.
  • If the AI download bar is still at the top, wait for it to finish; syncing starts by itself.
  • Press Sync now on Orders. The message next to the button says how far it got.
  • Still nothing? Look at Logs, or press Send logs to developer there.

An order is missing

Find its email on Inbox (try searching the order number) and label it. For orders older than a year, press Backfill whole inboxes.

An order has the wrong status

Correct it with the dropdown on Orders. The AI learns from it.

Gmail won't accept the app password

App passwords only exist once 2-Step Verification is on. Make sure you created it while signed in to the same Gmail account you're adding. Spaces in the code don't matter.

Syncing is slow

Each email takes the AI a few seconds. Try an 8B model or an MLX model on Compare models; they're faster, and Compare shows how accurate they are on your own mail.

Discord checkouts don't come in

Check that Message Content Intent is on for your bot, that the bot is in the server, and that the channels are ticked on Setup → Discord. A channel with a problem shows the error in its Last read column.

Updates fail

Updates need an internet connection. Press Check for update on Setup → Updates; if it shows an error, download the logs and send them over.

Where is my data?

In ~/Library/Application Support/BotTrack on this Mac: your settings, database, logs and AI models. Updates never touch it. Card numbers are encrypted with a key kept in your Keychain.

Does any of this cost money?

No. The AI runs on your Mac, Gmail is read with a free app password, tracking links go to the carriers' free pages, and Discord, the phone link and the carrier keys are free.
